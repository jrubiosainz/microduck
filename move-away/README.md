# move-away — moving aside without losing sight of the person

The final validated implementation of the avoidance behavior (2026-08-31). Earlier
iterations of this behavior — the original maneuver and the first onboard-camera
version — were consolidated into this folder; they remain reachable in the Git
history.

The duck sees a person approaching through its head camera, walks straight backward,
turns about 90°, and continues backing up on the new heading until it clears the
person's path:

```
IDLE → RETREAT → TURN → CLEAR → DONE
```

On top of that validated maneuver it adds an independent gaze layer that aims the
head's yaw and pitch toward the person throughout the sequence. The video lasts
22 seconds: the person keeps walking for 3 seconds longer than in the earlier
19-second version.

No new policy was trained. The stock `alpha_walking.onnx` policy is driven by a
velocity command generated by a state machine.

## Validated results

- **Visibility:** `1100/1100` control steps; `lost_steps=0`.
- **Maximum angular error:** `1.2°` from the camera center.
- **Motion:** same transition sequence and trajectory as the validated baseline.
- **Final stability:** upright in `DONE`, with `trunk z=0.116`.
- **Video:** 22 s, 960×640, 50 fps, H.264.
- **PiP:** 225×165 px, upper-right corner.

## Running it

From a `microduck_rl` checkout with its environment (`mujoco`, `onnxruntime`,
`imageio`, Pillow, and ffmpeg):

```bash
python scripts/render_phase1.py \
  --seconds 22 --out /tmp/move-away-frames --fps 50
ffmpeg -framerate 50 -i /tmp/move-away-frames/f%05d.png \
  -c:v libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart \
  media/move-away-head-tracking.mp4
```

Place `assets/scene_yield.xml` under `src/mjlab_microduck/robot/microduck/`
and `onnx/alpha_walking.onnx` under `onnx/`.

## How tracking works

The direction from the camera to the person's torso is projected onto the real
optical axes of `head_camera`. A visual servo corrects:

- `head_yaw`: horizontal error;
- `head_pitch`: vertical error.

The camera follows MuJoCo's convention (`-Z` is forward and `+Y` is up).

**Optical-frame correction:** the `head_camera` quaternion exported by the
upstream MJCF does not follow that convention, so the initial render showed
internal/side geometry instead of the person. At runtime the script applies a
−90° local rotation around Z (`[√½, 0, 0, −√½]`), making `-Z` point forward and
aligning the image vertically with the head. Detection then uses those same
optical axes and the PiP's real frustum, so the image and the visibility status
agree throughout the turn. Visibility is also checked through a scene ray cast.

### Separating gaze from locomotion

The head represents a large fraction of microduck's total mass. Two experiments
that moved it physically while walking caused the robot to fall, even when the
policy's stabilizing output was preserved. The locomotion ONNX policy was not
trained to compensate for an external head trajectory.

This version therefore uses an **independent kinematic gaze layer**:

1. locomotion physics and policy inference advance in the original `MjData`,
   unchanged from the stable baseline;
2. perception and rendering use an isolated `MjData` copy whose head yaw and
   pitch are aimed at the person;
3. that pose is never fed back into the locomotion dynamics.

This mirrors the proper separation between gaze and locomotion controllers on
the physical robot, and avoids claiming dynamic stability that the current
policy cannot actually provide.

## Perception

Perception uses a **ray cast** from the head camera to the person, not
segmentation. Segmentation was unusable because the camera *site* sits INSIDE the
duck's jaw geometry, so every ray/pixel hits `jaw_soft` first and the person is
never visible. The ray cast skips the duck's own bodies and answers the actual
question: is the person inside the field of view and unobstructed?

**The duck faces +X.** An earlier version moved the person toward −X after
misreading the camera matrix, placing them back-to-back.

## MEASURED parameters — do not change blindly

These findings came from repeatedly breaking the simulation. They are
observations, not cosmetic tuning:

- **Yaw authority only exists WHILE walking.** At rest, a `wz` command has almost
  no effect.
- **The sign of `wz` is INVERTED:** positive `wz` produces *negative* yaw velocity.
  This is why the control law includes a minus sign.
- **The useful `vx` window is extremely narrow:** below about −0.30 the gait does
  not engage and the duck steps in place; above about −0.33 it accumulates drift
  and falls over.
  - `VX_RETREAT = -0.28` → holds the heading within 1.5° over 5 seconds.
  - `VX_ROTATE = -0.31` → beyond the threshold, it self-rotates at about 110°/s.
- **Heading must use closed-loop control in EVERY walking state.** Leaving
  `wz = 0.00` during retreat was the real cause of the “turns the other way” bug:
  the gait is asymmetric and rotates on its own unless opposed (measured: −52°
  at t=9 s and +117° at t=12 s with `wz` exactly zero).
- `YAW_KP = 0.9`, `WZ_MAX = 0.20`. A gain of 0.6 loses control and tips the robot.
- `TURN_CUT = 45°` — rotation is stopped early because momentum carries it to
  about 90°.
- `CMD_TAU = 0.08` — command filter. A value of 0.25 is too slow to start walking.
- `RETREAT_HOLD = 5.0 s`, `CLEAR_HOLD = 2.5 s`.

### Reaction distance

Reaction triggers at `RETREAT_D = 1.15 m` with the person starting at
`x0 = 1.60 m`. An earlier version placed the person at 1.40 m and triggered at
0.95 m; adding the same 20 cm to both values makes the duck start at exactly the
same temporal phase of the ONNX policy while the person is still 20 cm farther
away, which preserves the stable trajectory. Changing only the activation time
altered the gait phase, and some tests over-rotated or fell over.
